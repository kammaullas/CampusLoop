# Phase 1 — Growth Strategy Brief: CampusLoop

## 1. Primary Student Segment

**Who:** Final-year engineering students (2026/2027 batch) at mid-tier private engineering colleges in India, currently in their 7th or 8th semester.

**Motivations:**
- Résumé anxiety: placement season is imminent or underway; they need a tangible project to discuss in interviews.
- AI curiosity gap: they've heard about AI everywhere but haven't built anything hands-on.
- Low-risk learning: a free, 60-minute commitment feels safe — it's not a 3-month course.
- Peer validation: if their classmates or a trusted class representative shares it, it feels credible.

**Barriers:**
- Skepticism about "free workshops" — they've been spammed by dozens of edtech companies.
- Time pressure: exams, projects, placement prep.
- Fear of being sold to (upsell anxiety).
- "I'm not an AI/ML person" self-selection out.

**Trigger Events:**
- A friend or class representative shares it in their section WhatsApp group.
- They see 3+ peers from their own college have registered (social proof).
- A campus ambassador frames it as "interview prep" not "AI course."

**Reason to Register:**
> "In 60 minutes, I get a working AI project I can show in my next interview — and it's free."

This is the **single sentence** the campaign must communicate. Every message, page, and share text reduces to this.

---

## 2. Core Conversion Insight

The workshop title is *"Build Your First AI Project in 60 Minutes."* The conversion insight is not "AI is cool" — it's **outcome certainty in a time-boxed commitment**.

Final-year students are risk-averse with their time. They will register when they believe:
1. **Outcome is concrete:** "a working AI project" — not "learn AI concepts."
2. **Time cost is bounded:** 60 minutes, not "a multi-week program."
3. **Barrier to entry is zero:** free, no prerequisites, no setup.
4. **Social proof is local:** "17 students from your college have already registered."

The registration page must **lead with the outcome** (project you'll build), not with the brand (NxtWave), the instructor, or generic AI hype.

## 3. Prioritised 7-Day Campaign Design

### Channel Selection Rationale

| Priority | Channel | Why |
|----------|---------|-----|
| **P0** | Class Rep WhatsApp Groups | Highest trust, highest density. One message reaches 60–80 students. |
| **P1** | Campus Ambassadors (personal referral) | Adds social proof + referral tracking. |
| **P2** | College Club Posts (coding/tech clubs) | Reaches self-selected tech-interested students. |
| **P3** | Instagram Reels/Stories (organic) | Low-cost amplification if content is good. |

### What We Deliberately Avoid (Section 9)
- **Paid Instagram/Facebook ads:** ₹2,000 budget yields ~2,000–4,000 impressions at best; terrible CPR for this audience. Not worth it.
- **LinkedIn outreach:** Final-year students are not active on LinkedIn in a way that converts to workshop signups.
- **Email campaigns:** We have no opt-in email list to start with.
- **Twitter/X:** Negligible penetration among this segment.
- **Building a chatbot or AI assistant:** Over-engineered for a 7-day sprint; adds complexity without proportional registrations.
- **Bulk SMS:** Legally and ethically problematic; no phone number list available.

### 7-Day Execution Schedule

| Day | Focus | Key Actions |
|-----|-------|-------------|
| **D1** | Setup & Seed | Launch registration page. Recruit 10 campus ambassadors from 10 colleges. Seed demo data. Test referral flow end-to-end. |
| **D2** | First Wave | Ambassadors share in their section WhatsApp groups (morning). Monitor registrations hourly. Target: 50 registrations. |
| **D3** | Social Proof Push | Share college-specific registration counts back to ambassadors ("23 students from [College] registered!"). Target: 100 cumulative. |
| **D4** | Club Amplification | Campus tech/coding clubs post in their groups and Instagram stories. Target: 200 cumulative. |
| **D5** | Mid-Campaign Check | Evaluate conversion rates. If behind, activate contingency: direct DM outreach by ambassadors to 5 friends each. Target: 300 cumulative. |
| **D6** | Urgency Push | "Only 2 days left" messaging. Leaderboard update to ambassadors. Target: 420 cumulative. |
| **D7** | Final Push | Last-day urgency + "X spots remaining" framing. Ambassadors do one final share. Target: 500 cumulative. |

## 4. Quantified Acquisition Model

### Assumptions (stated explicitly)

| Assumption | Value | Basis |
|------------|-------|-------|
| Avg. students per WhatsApp section group | 70 | Typical Indian engineering section size |
| Message open/view rate in WhatsApp group | 60% | WhatsApp group messages are usually seen, but not all acted upon |
| Click-through rate from WhatsApp message | 12% | Higher than email/social because it's a trusted peer sharing; conservative vs. industry 15-25% for personal shares |
| Landing page → Registration conversion | 35% | Free workshop with clear outcome; but some drop-off from skepticism. Industry benchmark for free webinars: 30-50% |
| Campus ambassadors recruited | 10 | Across 10 different colleges |
| WhatsApp groups per ambassador | 3 | Their own section + 1-2 friend groups or club groups |
| Club channels reached | 5 | Tech/coding clubs at 5 colleges |
| Members per club channel | 150 | Typical active membership |
| Organic Instagram reach per story/reel | 200 | Typical for student club accounts |

### Honest Assessment of the Arithmetic

**The base case yields ~140 registrations, not 500.**

To reach 500, we need one or more of these scaling levers:

| Scaling Lever | Multiplier Needed | Feasibility | How to Validate |
|---------------|-------------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **More ambassadors:** 30 instead of 10 (covering 30 colleges) | ~3× on Channel 1 & 2 → adds ~200 registrations | Medium — requires active recruitment | Recruit in batches of 5; measure per-ambassador yield |
| **Higher CTR** through better messaging (A/B tested) | 12% → 18% → adds ~50 registrations | Medium — depends on message quality | Test 2 message variants on Day 2; measure by Day 3 |
| **Higher conversion rate** through better landing page | 35% → 50% → adds ~60 registrations | Medium — depends on page quality | Track page visits vs. registrations; iterate on Day 3 |
| **Larger WhatsApp groups** (batch groups, hostel groups, year groups) | Reach 5,000+ instead of 2,100 | High if ambassadors have access | Ask ambassadors to list all available groups on Day 1 |

**Realistic Stretch Target:** With 25–30 active ambassadors across 25+ colleges and optimised messaging, **300–500 registrations** is achievable but not guaranteed. The plan treats 500 as a goal to pursue aggressively, not a commitment.

### De-duplication Method

| Metric | How Measured | Uniqueness |
|--------|-------------|------------|
| Gross Reach | Sum of group sizes × message waves | May double-count students in multiple groups |
| Unique Reach | Gross reach × 0.85 (15% estimated overlap) | Still an estimate |
| Clicks | Unique UTM-tagged link clicks (one per ambassador/channel) | Can still have repeat clicks from same person |
| Unique Visitors | Clicks × 0.90 (10% cross-channel dedup) | Approximation; true dedup requires cookie/fingerprint |
| Registrations | Form submissions deduplicated by email | **Exact — enforced by system** |
| Verified Unique Registrations | Registrations with valid email format + unique email constraint | **Exact — enforced by database** |

## 8. Validation Strategy (How a Strong Intern Would Test This)

> **Principle:** Never trust AI-generated conversion estimates. Validate with the smallest possible experiment before scaling.

### Experiment 1: Message CTR Test (Day 1-2, Cost: ₹0)
- **Hypothesis:** WhatsApp group message CTR is ≥10%.
- **Method:** Send to 3 WhatsApp groups (one per message variant). Use unique UTM links per group. Measure clicks after 24 hours.
- **Decision:** If CTR < 5%, rewrite copy before scaling to all groups. If CTR > 15%, scale aggressively.

### Experiment 2: Landing Page Conversion Test (Day 1-2, Cost: ₹0)
- **Hypothesis:** Landing page converts ≥30% of visitors to registrations.
- **Method:** Drive first 50 visitors from Experiment 1. Measure registrations.
- **Decision:** If conversion < 20%, simplify the form (remove optional fields, shorten copy). If > 40%, the page is working — focus on reach.

### Experiment 3: Ambassador Yield Test (Day 2-3, Cost: ₹0)
- **Hypothesis:** Each active ambassador generates ≥10 registrations in their first 48 hours.
- **Method:** Track registrations per ambassador via referral links.
- **Decision:** If average yield < 5, the ambassador model is too weak — pivot to club-based distribution. If > 15, double down on ambassador recruitment.

### Experiment 4: Referral Virality Test (Day 3-4, Cost: ₹0)
- **Hypothesis:** ≥10% of registrants share their referral link, generating ≥0.3 additional registrations per share.
- **Method:** Track share button clicks and downstream registrations.
- **Decision:** If viral coefficient < 0.05, remove referral complexity and focus on direct ambassador outreach.

| Don't Do | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Paid social ads with ₹2,000 | At ₹5–15 CPM, we get 150–400 impressions. Negligible. |

| Deploy to AWS/GCP with CI/CD | Local demo + simple deployment instructions are sufficient for the challenge |

## 10. Proposed Technical Architecture

### Stack Decision

| Layer | Choice | Rationale |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| Frontend | **React (Vite)** | Fast to build, component-based, great for SPA with multiple views |
| Backend | **Express.js (Node)** | Lightweight, same language as frontend, sufficient for this scope |
| Database | **SQLite (via better-sqlite3)** | Zero infrastructure, file-based persistence, perfect for local demo. Supports unique constraints for dedup. |
| Styling | **Vanilla CSS** | Per project guidelines; no Tailwind unless requested |
| Deployment | **Local dev server** | Primary mode. Optional: Railway/Render free tier instructions in README |
| QR Codes | **qrcode npm package** | Generates QR codes client-side for referral links |

### Architecture Diagram

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 React SPA (Vite)             │
│  ┌──────────┐ ┌──────────┐ ┌──────────────┐ │
│  │Register  │ │Referral  │ │Admin         │ │
│  │Page      │ │Share Page│ │Dashboard     │ │
│  └──────────┘ └──────────┘ └──────────────┘ │
└───────────────────┬─────────────────────────┘
                    │ REST API
┌───────────────────┴─────────────────────────┐
│            Express.js API Server             │
│  ┌──────────┐ ┌──────────┐ ┌──────────────┐ │
│  │/api/     │ │/api/     │ │/api/admin/*  │ │
│  │register  │ │referrals │ │(auth-gated)  │ │
│  └──────────┘ └──────────┘ └──────────────┘ │
└───────────────────┬─────────────────────────┘
                    │
┌───────────────────┴─────────────────────────┐
│         SQLite (campus_loop.db)              │
│  Tables: registrations, ambassadors,        │
│          experiment_log, campaign_sources    │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Key Design Decisions

1. **Admin auth:** Simple token-based auth (env variable `ADMIN_TOKEN`). Not production-grade, but prevents casual access and demonstrates the principle. No "security by obscurity."

2. **Dedup:** `UNIQUE` constraint on `email` column in SQLite. Server returns a clear "already registered" response — not a silent success.

3. **Referral tracking:** URL parameter `?ref=ambassador_id&src=whatsapp_club_name` is captured on page load, stored in React state, and submitted with registration. Attribution is preserved through the entire flow.

4. **Demo mode:** A `/api/admin/seed-demo` endpoint populates the database with clearly-labelled synthetic data. A banner on the dashboard indicates "Demo Mode — X synthetic records loaded." A `/api/admin/reset` endpoint clears all data.

5. **CSV export:** Server-side endpoint that returns `Content-Type: text/csv` with proper headers. No client-side CSV generation.

## Summary: The Growth Hypothesis

> **If** we recruit 20–30 campus ambassadors across 20+ colleges, give them tracked referral links and optimised WhatsApp messages, and run a 7-day campaign with daily feedback loops and message iteration —
>
> **Then** we can achieve 300–500 verified unique registrations for a free 60-minute AI workshop,
>
> **Because** final-year engineering students register for concrete, time-boxed outcomes when recommended by a trusted peer, and our system makes it easy to share, track, and optimise that recommendation at scale.

This hypothesis is testable by Day 3. If early data contradicts it, we pivot — not pretend.

---

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The 500-registration target is a goal, not a guarantee.** The acquisition model shows that reaching 500 requires scaling beyond the base case (more ambassadors, higher CTR, or both). The plan is designed to find and exploit the highest-yield channel within the first 3 days, then double down. Honest reporting of actual results is more valuable than inflated projections.
